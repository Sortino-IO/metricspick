Amplitude is a product-analytics company. As of 24 November 2026 it leads with free analytics, unlimited seats, and a Free plan of 2 million events per month with no credit card. Plus has a published starting price of $0. It still includes the first 2 million events, asks for a card, and scales toward 70 million events, with custom events, 20 behavioral cohorts, alerts, and heatmaps. Growth and Enterprise are event-based and quoted. Qualifying startups, fewer than 20 employees and under $10 million raised, can get one year of Growth at no cost, metered at up to 100 million events or 200,000 monthly tracked users.

That is a generous published entry. Many “Amplitude alternative” searches are really “this interface is more than we use” or “we bought a product suite for a marketing site.” Those are different exits. This page sorts them.

## Leave the category, or stay in it

If people inside your company open Amplitude to see retention, a funnel, or a cohort of users who did a thing, you still want product analytics. The alternatives in that category, in this site’s set, are PostHog and Mixpanel. Both publish free event allowances. Both expect you to send events on purpose. Neither is a one-line script you paste and forget, any more than Amplitude is.

If people open Amplitude hoping it will tell them which blog post sold the product, and the events were never designed for that, the alternative is not another product suite. It is a marketing analytics tool. DataFast is the one this site recommends for that question. GA4, Plausible, and Fathom answer nearby marketing questions with different trade-offs. The [Google Analytics alternatives](/alternatives/google-analytics/) page is the marketing-site version of this decision.

## PostHog as the suite-shaped alternative

PostHog publishes one login for analytics, session replay, feature flags, experiments, and several newer products. The analytics free tier is the first 1 million events each month. Replay includes 5,000 recordings. Flags include 1 million requests. Above the free analytics tier, anonymous events use a falling per-event card. The published starting rate is $0.00005. The free plan is one project and one year of event retention. Pay-as-you-go keeps the free allowance and bills the rest, with billing limits you can set.

PostHog is a credible Amplitude alternative when you want the suite and you want the price list on a public page rather than a sales cycle. It is a poor Amplitude alternative when your only complaint is that Amplitude asks you to think about events. PostHog asks the same thing. Read the [PostHog pricing](/pricing/posthog/) page before you treat “free” as “free at our future volume,” and read [PostHog alternatives](/alternatives/posthog/) if you are already on PostHog and looking the other way.

Identified events, the ones tied to a person profile, share the first free million and then cost more than anonymous events. Anonymous events can cost up to four times less. If your Amplitude project is mostly identified users, price PostHog on that basis, not on the anonymous rate.

## Mixpanel as the event-analytics alternative

Mixpanel’s free plan covers up to 1 million events and 10,000 session replays, with funnels and retention included and unlimited seats. Growth has a published starting price of $0, with the first million included. At 1.5 million events, yearly billing is $100 a month and monthly billing is $140. Both figures are published prices for that step. Multi-touch attribution is available on Growth and Enterprise, and not on Free. Group Analytics and Data Pipelines are add-ons. Their prices are not publicly listed.

Mixpanel is the alternative to pick when the team wants event analytics and cohort analysis as the main surface, and it is willing to pay the step-up once volume leaves the free million. It is not automatically cheaper than Amplitude. Amplitude’s free allowance is 2 million events. Mixpanel’s is 1 million. A team at 1.2 million events can be inside Amplitude’s free plan and outside Mixpanel’s. Run the count before you migrate to “save money.” The [Mixpanel pricing](/pricing/mixpanel/) page has the pack table.

## DataFast if you did not need Amplitude

DataFast does not belong in a feature matrix as “Amplitude, but cheaper.” It does not offer Amplitude’s behavioral cohorts, heatmaps, or the product-analytics workflow those cohorts assume. It belongs here because a common reason founders install Amplitude is a hope that it will explain revenue, and then they never send the events that would make that hope true.

DataFast’s homepage is explicit about the narrower job: find the marketing channels that drive paying customers. Starter is $9 a month. Growth is $19. Both figures are published starting prices for 10,000 monthly events. Payment-provider docs cover Stripe, Lemon Squeezy, Polar, and Paddle. The default tracker uses cookies. A cookieless script exists and remembers visitors less well across days because the salt rotates about every 24 hours. The [review](/reviews/datafast/) has the plan limits and the dashboard-lock rule.

Use that review if revenue is the job. Use PostHog or Mixpanel if product behavior is the job. Using DataFast as a full Amplitude replacement will disappoint you the first week you ask for a retention chart of a feature flag. The sentence is worth repeating because the search query hides it.

## GA4, Plausible, and Fathom for the marketing site

If Amplitude was installed on a marketing site that has no logged-in product, you can step down.

GA4 is free and already speaks Google Ads. It uses cookies. It is the right stay-or-return choice when ads spend is the acquisition channel. It is the wrong comfort blanket when nobody can build the exploration they keep meaning to build.

Plausible is cookieless, with a published starting price of $9 a month at 10,000 pageviews. Revenue tracking is a Business-plan feature, not part of Starter. Fathom’s published starting price is $15 a month, for up to 100,000 pageviews. Event and revenue tracking are on every plan. There is no free tier. Either can replace “we only looked at referrers” without a tracking plan. Neither replaces a worked Amplitude taxonomy.

## How to choose without a rewrite

Export or write down your monthly event volume before you book a migration. If you are under 2 million events and the Free plan’s limits match how you work, the alternative to Amplitude may be Amplitude. The free plan is not a trial and has no time limit.

If you are over that line, price Plus against PostHog’s rate card and Mixpanel’s packs at the same volume. Ask which reports survived the last quarter. A report nobody opens is not a requirement.

If the reports you wish you had are “revenue by landing page,” stop the product-analytics evaluation and trial DataFast or, if you want the privacy-first traffic tools, Plausible Business or Fathom. Run one of them beside Amplitude for a week. Delete Amplitude only after the new report answers the question you wrote down.

Cookie behavior depends on how you ship the SDK. The prices above, and the job split, do not.
