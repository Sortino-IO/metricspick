Fathom’s pricing page is built to be read in a minute. As of 24 November 2026, the published starting price is $15 a month, for up to 100,000 monthly pageviews and 50 sites. At that step, Standard is $15 a month and Business is $39. Both figures are published prices for up to 100,000 monthly pageviews. Standard includes the analytics features, 50 sites, email support, and an API allowance. Business adds seats, roles, an audit log, and a higher API limit. Traffic growth does not force you from Standard to Business. You change plans when other people need logins. Custom plans cover volumes past 25 million pageviews, invoicing, and an SLA.

Every plan includes event and revenue tracking, UTM campaign tracking, email reports, an API, bot filtering, and history for as long as you subscribe. A cookie banner is not required. There is no free plan. Fathom sells software rather than data, and does not want the free tier’s infrastructure bill. The trial is 7 days, a card is required, and Fathom emails you before billing starts. Yearly prices are ten times monthly. Extra seats are $10 a month or $100 a year. Extra sites are packs of 50 at the same $10 or $100.

The pageview slider goes higher than 100,000. Prices for those higher steps are not listed here. If your site is obviously larger than 100,000 pageviews, open the slider before you compare a $15 screenshot with another vendor’s $9 screenshot.

## Plausible, feature by feature enough to decide

Plausible is the alternative that feels most similar: a focused site-analytics product, a privacy pitch, and a public ladder. The ladder is more fully published. At 10,000 pageviews, Plausible Starter is $9. Fathom’s published starting price is $15. At 100,000 pageviews, Plausible Starter is $19 and Fathom Standard is $15. Both figures are published prices at that volume. Plausible’s docs describe Starter as one site. Fathom includes 50. A freelancer with twenty client sites should not stop at the headline price.

Revenue tracking is the other inversion. Fathom lists it on all plans. Plausible’s docs send you to Business for revenue tracking, funnels, and journeys. A team that wants revenue and wants the cheapest plan may prefer Fathom. A team that wants the lowest price at very small traffic and does not need revenue may prefer Plausible Starter. The [Plausible alternatives](/alternatives/plausible/) article carries Plausible’s full slider.

Trials differ. Plausible documents 30 days and no card. Fathom documents 7 days and a card. If you hate entering a card to look, that alone can decide the trial, even when the annual price would have gone the other way.

## DataFast when the revenue line is why you are shopping

Fathom includes revenue tracking. DataFast is organized around it. The homepage, the terms, and the attribution guide all talk about payments, channels, and pages. The published starting price is $9 a month, for 10,000 events and one site. That is a tighter site limit than Fathom’s 50, and a smaller allowance than Fathom’s 100,000 pageviews. A small marketing site with real payments can still be cheaper on DataFast. A studio with many quiet sites can be cheaper on Fathom before revenue features even enter the chat. Count sites and count events. The units are not identical, because DataFast counts payment events and goals in the same bucket as pageviews.

DataFast’s cookieless mode is optional. Fathom’s default is that a banner is not required. If a cookieless default is the requirement, read DataFast’s cookieless doc before you treat the two as the same privacy product. Long journeys attribute less completely without the cookie. The [cookieless guide](/guides/cookieless-analytics/) puts that limit in plain language. The [review](/reviews/datafast/) recommends DataFast for revenue attribution, and it does not replace PostHog, Mixpanel, or Amplitude.

Use DataFast if you will connect a payment provider and look at revenue by source every week. Use Fathom if you will hand a client a simple dashboard and you want revenue available without a plan upgrade. Those can both be true for different sites you own. They are not a ranking of virtue.

## GA4 and PostHog

GA4 is the free alternative and the cookie-based one. Choose it when Google Ads reporting is worth more than Fathom’s interface. Leave it when the team has already decided the GA4 interface is the problem. The [GA4 alternatives](/alternatives/google-analytics/) page includes Fathom in that wider set.

PostHog is not a Fathom alternative in the way Plausible is. It is a product suite with a free million events and a usage curve. Adopt it when the new requirement is replay, flags, or in-app funnels. Adopting it to avoid Fathom’s $15 is a category error that creates a different bill. The [PostHog pricing](/pricing/posthog/) page shows the curve. The [attribution guide](/guides/marketing-attribution-tools/) shows where Fathom’s revenue line sits next to GA4, Plausible Business, and DataFast.

## Prices above 100,000 pageviews

Email yourself last month’s pageviews before you believe any “from $15” sentence, including this one. If you are under 100,000, the Standard and Business prices above are the ones at that step. If you are over it, the slider on Fathom’s site is your actual price. Compare that number with the Plausible row for the same volume and with the DataFast row for your events, including payments.

Then run the trial that matches the risk you care about. Card or no card. Revenue line or not. Fifty sites or one. The rest of the marketing adjectives are similar enough that the invoice and the job should decide.

## A note on what “simple” costs later

Fathom’s pitch is that you will not outgrow the analytics features by accident, because Standard and Business share them. The thing you outgrow is pageviews, sites, or seats. That is a kinder model than a tool that hides revenue behind a higher plan, and it is a harsher model than a tool with a permanent free tier. Write the outgrow path down while you are still on the trial.

If pageviews double, you will move along Fathom’s slider, and that price is the one on Fathom’s site. If headcount doubles, Business at $39, plus $10 per extra seat, is the path at the 100,000 pageview step. If the number of client sites doubles past 50, packs of 50 sites at $10 are the path. None of those jumps require you to relearn the product. They do require a budget line.

Plausible’s outgrow path is different. You may change tier and you may change plan, from Starter to Growth to Business, when you want sharing or revenue. DataFast’s outgrow path is the event ladder, and a payment-heavy month moves you faster because payments count as events. GA4’s outgrow path is not a price. It is complexity, or a sales conversation about Analytics 360, which is quote-only.

Put those paths next to each other in a single note before you cancel Fathom to save $6 a month. If Fathom changes the $15 step, this article should change with it. As of 24 November 2026, the floor is $15, Business at 100,000 pageviews is $39, and higher slider prices stay on Fathom’s site.
