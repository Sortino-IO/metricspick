Mixpanel’s pricing page, read on 24 November 2026, sells three plans: Free, Growth, and Enterprise. Free is up to 1 million events a month, 10,000 session replays, up to 10 active feature flags, up to 1,000 monthly experiment users, and funnels and retention, with unlimited seats and no card. Growth starts at $0, includes that first million, and then sells packs. The page’s yearly column shows 1.5 million events at $100 a month and 2 million at $185. The monthly column shows $140 and $259 at those same steps. Enterprise is a conversation, up to a published ceiling of a trillion events, which is a way of saying “call us.”

A startup program on the same page offers the first year free to companies founded less than five years ago, with up to $8 million in funding, that have not redeemed another offer. The included volumes on that offer are large. Read the current page before you plan around them. Programs change.

People search for Mixpanel alternatives for three reasons: the step from 1 million to 1.5 million events felt steep, the product is more than the team uses, or attribution still does not look like the spreadsheet they wanted. Each reason has a different shortlist.

## If the bill is the reason

Do the volume check first. Amplitude’s free plan, on its pricing page the same day, is 2 million events a month. A project at 1.4 million events can be a Mixpanel Growth customer and an Amplitude Free customer. That is not a reason to like Amplitude’s interface. It is a reason to refuse a migration story that skips the allowance.

PostHog’s first million analytics events are free, then anonymous events are $0.00005 for the next million. A rough public-rate example, not an invoice: the half million events between 1.0 and 1.5 million anonymous events cost about $25 on that rate, before replay or flags. Mixpanel’s published pack at 1.5 million is $100 a month on yearly billing. The products are not the same, so the cheaper arithmetic can still be the wrong product. It does mean “Mixpanel is the only grown-up option” is false at small volumes.

If you are far above 20 million events, Mixpanel tells you to contact sales, and PostHog and Amplitude will also be in a custom conversation. This guide’s tables stop being the decision. A procurement comparison is.

Group Analytics and Data Pipelines are add-ons on paid Mixpanel plans. We did not see a standalone dollar price for them in the captured pricing text. If your Mixpanel bill is mostly an add-on, ask for that line before you assume the event pack is the problem. The [pricing guide](/pricing/mixpanel/) repeats the packs we could verify.

## If you still want product analytics

PostHog and Amplitude are the alternatives that stay in category.

PostHog is the wider suite: replay allowances, flags, experiments, and a public rate card with billing limits. It is also more product than Mixpanel in places you may not want to operate. The [PostHog alternatives](/alternatives/posthog/) page is there if you arrive and want to leave again. The [PostHog vs GA4](/compare/posthog-vs-google-analytics/) page is there if the other tool in the bake-off is Google.

Amplitude is the larger free event tier and a Plus plan that starts at $0 with a card, custom events, a limited number of behavioral cohorts, and two years of retention. Growth is quoted. The startup year of Growth has its own eligibility rules, which are not the same as Mixpanel’s startup rules. Do not assume a yes from one program is a yes from the other.

Moving between these three is a tracking-plan move. Budget a week to confirm the same five events arrive with the same names, and another week before anyone trusts a funnel. The reports that matter are the ones a human opens. Recreate those first.

## If you wanted revenue by channel

Mixpanel can represent a purchase as an event. Growth adds multi-touch attribution on the pricing matrix we read. Free does not. Campaign reporting is listed on the plans more broadly. That is real product surface. It is still an event system. Someone has to decide what a campaign property looks like and keep it on the events.

DataFast is the tool this site recommends when you want that answer without adopting a product-analytics practice. Starter starts at $9 per month for 10,000 events. The docs connect common payment providers. The [review](/reviews/datafast/) lists the ladder, the 14-day trial, and the rule that the dashboard locks at 100 percent of the event allowance while tracking continues. DataFast is not a full replacement for Mixpanel. Cohorts of feature usage, replay of a broken screen, and experiment users are Mixpanel’s neighborhood. If you use them, keep a product tool and add DataFast only on the marketing host.

Fathom lists event and revenue tracking on every paid plan and starts at $15 for up to 100,000 pageviews. Plausible points revenue tracking at the Business plan. GA4 is free and is the ads-shaped answer. The [attribution guide](/guides/marketing-attribution-tools/) lines these up without pretending they compute the same credit model. We do not publish a winner on attribution accuracy. We did not run that test.

## If Mixpanel was only on the marketing site

A marketing site that sends a pageview as a Mixpanel event will hit the event cap with ordinary traffic and will still feel like the wrong instrument. Plausible and Fathom price by pageviews and are built as site analytics. Plausible says it does not use cookies. Fathom says a cookie banner is not required. GA4 is the free option that uses cookies and speaks Google Ads.

The [GA4 alternatives](/alternatives/google-analytics/) page is the better article if Mixpanel was a detour and GA4 is what you are actually comparing. Use this page if the detour started from a real event taxonomy and you might still need one.

## A clean way to decide

Count last month’s events. List the five reports that were opened. If both the count and the reports fit Free, stop. If the reports are product reports, price PostHog and Amplitude at the same count and migrate one funnel before you migrate the company. If the reports are marketing reports, trial DataFast or a privacy-first site tool beside Mixpanel, and turn Mixpanel down only when the new report has been right once.

Affiliate links on this site point at DataFast and are labeled. They are not a reason to throw away a Mixpanel project that your product team uses every day. The [how we test](/how-we-test/) note is the boundary: public pages, a date, and no invented win rate.
