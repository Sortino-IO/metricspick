DataFast versus Google Analytics is usually asked by someone who is tired of GA4 and hopes a smaller tool will explain where the money came from. Sometimes that is the right hope. It is the wrong hope if the thing GA4 is doing is Google Ads. Standard GA4 is free. DataFast is a paid product with a narrow job. Those facts decide more installs than a feature checklist does.

This page was checked against public pages on 25 November 2026. We did not run one site in both tools. DataFast means the analytics product at [datafa.st](https://datafa.st/). The [review](/reviews/datafast/) says what it is not: not DataFast Networks in Arizona, and not Datafast S.A., the payments company in Ecuador.

## What you would be deleting

Google’s analytics marketing page says the product gives you the tools, free of charge, to understand the customer journey and improve marketing ROI. The same page lists Google Ads, Search Ads 360, Display & Video 360, Google Cloud, and Search Console among integrations. That is the stack people mean when they say they already have analytics. Standard GA4 does not have a monthly list price on that page because the standard product is described as free. Analytics 360 is the enterprise offer. We did not find a public 360 price, so this page does not invent one.

GA4’s standard web tag uses cookies. Leaving GA4 for a privacy pitch is a real project, and DataFast’s default script does not finish it, because that script uses cookies too. Plausible and Fathom lead with cookieless designs. They are the comparisons to read if the banner is the reason you want to leave, and revenue-by-provider is not.

## What DataFast adds, and what it costs

DataFast’s homepage sells one report: which marketing channels bring paying customers. You install a script and connect a payment provider. The homepage FAQ names Stripe, Lemon Squeezy, Polar, and Shopify, plus a Payment API. Starter is $9 a month at 10,000 events. Growth is $19 at that step. The [pricing page](/pricing/datafast/) has the rest of the ladder, the 14-day trial, and the point at which the dashboard locks. There is no ongoing free plan in the terms.

That $9 is worth it when GA4’s explorations are not showing you the payment and you are willing to connect the provider. It is not worth it as a way to stop paying for GA4, because standard GA4 is not a bill. You would be adding a bill to get a different report. The [“Is DataFast worth it?”](/reviews/datafast/) answer on the review is the short form of that test.

The default DataFast script is the accurate one for long journeys, and it may need a cookie banner of its own. The cookieless script is the one DataFast says is weaker when the sale takes weeks. If you keep GA4 and add DataFast, you now have two cookie scripts unless you choose the cookieless DataFast mode and accept the attribution limit. Write that down before you install both.

## When the pair is wrong

If the real comparison was “product suite versus GA4,” read [PostHog vs Google Analytics](/compare/posthog-vs-google-analytics/). PostHog’s pricing page lists 1 million analytics events, 5,000 session recordings, and 1 million feature-flag requests in the free allowances. DataFast does not sell that suite.

If the real comparison was “lighter site analytics versus GA4,” [DataFast vs Plausible](/compare/datafast-vs-plausible/) and [DataFast vs Fathom](/compare/datafast-vs-fathom/) are the privacy-first pair. DataFast belongs in the GA4 conversation when the missing number is revenue by source, not when any non-Google logo would do.
