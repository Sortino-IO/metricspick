DataFast pricing is a volume ladder, not a seat fee. Checked on 25 November 2026, the public homepage embeds monthly USD prices for two plan types, Starter and Growth, at nine event steps. The path `datafa.st/pricing` returned a 404 that day, as it did on 24 November. The cards on the homepage are what a buyer sees. The numbers in the table above are those embedded prices. They match the 24 November reading. Nothing in the dollar column moved.

This page is only the analytics plans for the product at datafa.st, Marc Lou’s web analytics tool. It is not a price list for DataFast Networks in Arizona or for Datafast S.A. in Ecuador. The [review](/reviews/datafast/) separates those names. If the price is right and the job is not, the [alternatives](/alternatives/datafast/) page is the shortlist, and the [Plausible](/compare/datafast-vs-plausible/) and [GA4](/compare/datafast-vs-google-analytics/) comparisons are the two closest forks. Links to datafa.st on this page are affiliate links.

## The monthly ladder

An event, in the terms effective 15 February 2026, is any point the script tracks: pageviews, payment events, free-trial signups, and custom goals. They count equally. They add up across every website on the account, inside the monthly billing cycle. A yearly subscription is still metered every month. You are not buying a year of events in one bucket.

The steps in the homepage data are 10,000, 100,000, 200,000, 500,000, 1 million, 2 million, 5 million, 10 million, and 10 million plus.

Starter monthly prices at those steps: $9, $19, $29, $49, $69, $89, $129, $169, $199.

Growth monthly prices: $19, $39, $59, $99, $139, $179, $259, $339, $399.

The homepage FAQ tells a new site to start at 10,000 events, a site with a couple of hundred visitors a day to look at 100,000 or 200,000, and a site with a thousand or more visitors a day to look at 1 million plus. That is their sizing advice, not a measurement we ran.

## Yearly billing

Yearly prices in the same data are ten times the monthly price. Each yearly object also stores a 12-month anchor, so the discount is two months. The homepage toggle says “2 months free.” A 10,000-event Starter year is $90, against an anchor of $108. A 10,000-event Growth year is $190, against $228. Multiply any monthly cell by ten if you pay yearly. Do not multiply by twelve.

## What the plan cards include

The feature ticks below were read on the 10,000-event cards. The homepage FAQ describes the same website, seat, and retention split without tying it to a volume step. The JavaScript limits for websites, team members, and social mentions are set on the plan type, not on the event step. We did not click every step to watch the ticks redraw.

Both cards include the event allowance shown, 100,000 AI bot requests, and “API, CLI & MCP.” The tooltip on the bot line says the 100,000 requests are per billing cycle, pooled across the account, and that an additional 1 million requests is $9 a month. The homepage config sets that pack at $9 and dates the enforcement start to 15 September 2026. That $9 is not the Starter plan. Normal analytics is a different meter from bot ingestion.

Starter, and not Growth, is ticked for 1 website, 1 team member, and 3 years of data retention. Social media mentions and X link attribution are crossed out on Starter.

Growth is ticked for 30 websites, 30 team members, and 5+ years of data retention, plus social mentions and X link attribution. The Starter-or-Growth FAQ says the same thing in prose: Starter for a solo founder, Growth for the site count, the seats, the longer retention, and those two features.

A different FAQ on the same homepage says you can have unlimited team members. The cards and the plan FAQ do not say that. They say 1 and 30. Treat the card on the tier you are about to pay for as the limit, and read the current FAQ before you invite a team on the strength of the word “unlimited.”

## Trial, lock, and cancellation

The terms say new users are eligible for a 14-day free trial. The homepage button says “Start 14-day free trial” and “$0.00 due today. No card required.” After the trial, a subscription is required. If you do not subscribe, the terms say the dashboard locks, tracking stops, and analytics data is deleted 7 days after the trial ends. If you cancel a paid subscription, tracking continues for 30 days and is then deleted.

At 75 percent of the event limit, the terms say DataFast emails you. At 100 percent, the dashboard locks until you upgrade to a plan that covers usage in that cycle. Events continue to be tracked while the dashboard is locked. You cannot select a plan with a lower limit than the usage you already have. You can downgrade later if usage drops, but the terms say you still cover the current cycle.

The terms also say reports reflect data as processed and stored, and that DataFast does not provide consolidated archive exports or raw event logs. Plan the export while the account is alive.
